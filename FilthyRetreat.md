GLOBAL MARKETING MARKUP RULES
1. Cally's voice is not the same as body copy
Use three recognisable registers:
MARKETING COPY
Clear. Desirable. Understandable.
CALLY
Personal. Direct. Mischievous. Human.
CALLY VANDALISM
Rare. Handwritten. Interferes with something that was previously well behaved.
Do not make every line sound like Cally. Contrast gives her appearances power.
2. Do not overuse cheekiness
Cheekiness works because it breaks established restraint.
The high-value moments currently are:
you probably shouldn't. ♡
the itinerary vandalism
A lot of filthy. ♡
or I could. ♡
mostly.
Futanari Princess / meow.
“There aren't many.”
Do not add fifteen more.
3. Image placeholders are intentional design objects
Do not render broken-image icons or generic grey boxes.
A placeholder should clearly communicate to the creative team what needs to be produced.
Example:
IMAGE TO CREATE
Golden South African river scene
Late afternoon / energetic / warm
Human presence optional
Landscape orientation

Style it elegantly enough that the unfinished build remains reviewable.
4. Mobile is primary
Do not allow enormous photographs and pinned sections to create minutes of unnecessary scrolling.
The previous lesson stands:
no more symphony for the sake of symphony — this is a webpage selling a retreat.
Desktop may become expansive.
Mobile must remain immediate, tactile and easy to read.
5. Day → night is one continuous environmental story
This is not “light theme / dark theme.”
The visitor should feel:
morning/warmth → golden day → late afternoon → sunset → violet dusk → moonlit night → fire → dying embers → playful coloured night → morning.
Backgrounds must contain colour.
Never sterile white. Never featureless pure black.
6. Preserve existing commerce
This pass is frontend marketing and presentation.
Do not redesign:
pricing authority, enquiry storage, product/format data model, guest-count logic, backend contracts, admin workflow, holds, payment architecture or booking architecture unless a genuine incompatibility is discovered.
Report incompatibilities rather than “fixing” unrelated systems.
FINAL ACCEPTANCE TEST
Before calling the build complete, review the page on mobile and desktop and ask these questions in order:
Hero: Do I want to escape there?
Three Nights: Do I understand broadly what I'm getting?
Massage: Do I feel spoiled?
Day: Can I imagine actually enjoying three days here?
Sunset: Do I want to know what happens tonight?
Filthy Feast: Am I hungry?
Whisky: Am I curious?
Play: Do I want to meet this woman?
Cally: Do I understand why she's part of the value?
Morning: Does this feel comfortable enough that I could actually do it?
Offer: Do I know what I'm buying?
Enquiry: Can I say yes without friction?
Inner Sanctum: If I don't say yes today, do I still want to remain inside this world?
If any section cannot answer its question, fix the execution before adding more content.
The governing commercial principle for the entire implementation is:
MAKE THEM WANT TO BE THERE.
Not merely impressed by the website.
Not merely attracted to Cally.
Not merely curious about Filthy Princess.
Make them want those three nights.
